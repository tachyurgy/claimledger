Claim Ledger

event-sourced RCM
outpatient behavioral health · posting date

A working revenue-cycle workbench for an outpatient behavioral-health group. Every claim is an append-only event log — ClaimDrafted → ClaimScrubbed → ClaimSubmitted (837P) → ClaimAcknowledged (277CA) → RemittanceReceived (835) — and claim state is a left fold over that log, so the audit trail is free and any claim can be replayed to a prior state. Denials are triaged by expected recoverable dollars rather than raw balance, then split into a deterministic lane a machine can clear and a lane that genuinely needs a human.

Teal is the modeled recoverable portion; the dark remainder is what the playbook expects to lose. Sorting the book this way is what surfaces that a high-volume code like CO-45 is pure contractual noise while a mid-volume CO-197 is where the recoverable money actually sits.

How it is built. engine.js is a pure reducer: apply(state, event) → state with an explicit legal-transition table, so an out-of-order 835 raises InvalidTransition instead of silently walking a paid claim backwards. replay() rebuilds any claim from its log. The scrubber runs the pre-submission edits that keep a claim from being denied in the first place (POS 10 without modifier 95, missing diagnosis pointer, malformed NPI, timely-filing cliffs). No framework, no build step — open devtools and call ledger.replay().

Reference data is real: CARC/RARC adjustment codes from the X12 835 CAS and LQ segments, behavioral-health CPT codes with plausible allowed amounts, and payer-specific timely filing and appeal windows. Claim volume is synthetic and seeded — no patient data of any kind.
